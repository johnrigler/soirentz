# Soirentz

Soirentz is a ledger-oriented framework for religious and quasi-religious institutional identity.

The cleanest analogy is:

> **Soirentz is CertLedger for religion.**

Where CertLedger records lawyers, judges, cases, credentials, conduct, and institutional facts, Soirentz records religious organizations, stewards, ministers, participants, credentials, doctrine, governance, succession, work, compensation, attestations, and durable institutional history.

## Core terms

- **Soirism**: the broader religious/institutional framework.
- **Soirentz**: the person's or institution's accumulated journey/record through that framework.
- **Church of the Nones**: a public-facing social frame for people with no conventional religious affiliation who still want durable institutions, gathering, ritual, memory, service, succession, and compensated stewardship.

## Institutional premise

The goal is not a purely online "church" or a paper tax wrapper.

A serious implementation should have observable institutional reality:

- legal entity;
- governing documents;
- identifiable officers or stewards;
- recurring public meetings;
- a geographical place where people actually gather;
- records of decisions, duties, compensation, succession, and participation;
- a stated creed, philosophy, doctrine, or governing purpose appropriate to that community;
- a durable identity that can be independently verified.

An outside observer, donor, member, successor, or government agent should be able to determine that the institution is doing what it says it is doing.

## CertLedger analogy

Soirentz should make religious institutional claims inspectable rather than merely asserted.

Examples:

- Who is this organization?
- What denomination or tradition does it claim?
- Who currently controls it?
- How did control pass from one steward to another?
- What credentials does a minister or officer claim?
- Who attested those credentials?
- What work was performed?
- What compensation was authorized?
- What bylaws or doctrine were in force at a given time?
- Which wallet addresses belong to the organization?
- Which documents or public records support the claim?
- What happened when leadership changed?

The purpose is not to decide theological truth. It is to make institutional facts, claims, and provenance auditable.

## Denomination rather than non-denomination

Soirism can act as an institutional protocol that different communities inherit without losing their own identity.

A Baptist organization can use it while remaining Baptist.

A secular Church of the Nones can use it without pretending to hold Baptist theology.

An absurdist or fictional religious organization can use it under its own doctrine.

The protocol preserves the identity of the adopting community rather than flattening everything into "non-denominational."

## Ninth Baptist

Ninth Baptist is a concrete example.

A Baptist entity could adopt Soirism/Soirentz language in its bylaws and governance. Succession could be designed so future Baptist participants can inherit or assume stewardship through a documented process.

Important distinction:

- ordinary law creates the legal entity and whatever tax status it qualifies for;
- Soirentz records identity, governance, succession, duties, attestations, and history;
- the ledger does not itself create tax-exempt status.

A ledger message such as "HELLO IRS" can act as a durable index into the institution's public record, not as a legal incantation.

## Work and compensation

The framework assumes meaningful institutional work can be compensated.

Soirentz should make work legible:

- role;
- term;
- duties;
- attestations;
- comparable compensation;
- approvals;
- payments;
- results;
- succession.

The goal is not to disguise private profit as religion. The goal is to make institutional labor auditable and economically sustainable.

Any real-world compensation, housing allowance, charitable deduction, church classification, or other tax treatment must still satisfy the applicable law independently of the ledger.

## Ledger model

A Soirentz identity can accumulate:

- work history;
- credentials;
- relationships;
- testimony and attestations;
- governing roles;
- public service;
- authored artifacts;
- donations;
- compensation;
- succession events;
- doctrine and bylaw versions;
- organization-to-wallet attestations.

The chain is not the religion. It is the persistence and verification layer.

The design should avoid permanent dependence on any single chain. Solana, DigiByte, Litecoin, Dogecoin, Polygon, or future systems may all act as carriers if the protocol preserves a clear succession and verification rule.

## Church of the Nones

"Church of the Nones" describes the social problem cleanly.

People can leave inherited denominations without ceasing to need:

- community;
- ceremony;
- public gathering;
- education;
- mutual aid;
- mentorship;
- charitable activity;
- burial and memorial practices;
- institutional memory;
- succession;
- recognition of long-term service.

Soirism proposes that these functions can be rebuilt without requiring everyone to inherit the same metaphysics.

## Design principle

The institution should be unusually legible.

Instead of relying on religious opacity, Soirentz should make identity, governance, succession, work, money, and claims easier to audit than in a conventional institution.
